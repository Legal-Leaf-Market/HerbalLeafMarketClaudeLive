The same pouch, two rituals

Nothing changes about the herbs. What changes is which parts of them reach you, and that turns out to be most of the experience.

Smoked

Rolled, or packed in a pipe
  • The aromatic herbs lead. Damiana and mugwort are what you notice first.
  • Mullein does the work you do not taste: it is the filler that keeps a blend burning evenly instead of going out.
  • Flavour arrives immediately and finishes fast.
  • Grind coarse. A fine grind packs tight, restricts airflow and burns hot and harsh.

Brewed

Five to ten minutes, off the boil
  • The soft, sweet, grassy herbs lead instead. Raspberry leaf and mullein carry the cup.
  • The sharp aromatic edges stay behind in the pot rather than reaching you.
  • Flavour builds slowly and keeps developing as it cools.
  • Mugwort-heavy blends turn bitter if you walk away from them. Watch the clock.

Which one should you start with

Brew it. Two reasons: the cup tells you what is actually in the blend more clearly than smoke does, and if you are moving away from cigarettes then a blend in a pot is a different habit rather than the same habit with different contents. Roll it once you know what you like.

The herbs, and what each one is doing

Read a blend's ingredient list and you can usually predict how it will behave. These are the herbs that turn up most, and the job each one is doing in the mix. Flavour and function, not effect.

Reading a ratio

Ingredient lists usually run heaviest first. A blend that opens with mullein or raspberry leaf is a gentle one built to be a vehicle. A blend that opens with damiana or mugwort is built to taste of something, and will be much more assertive in both preparations. If lavender or rose is anywhere in the first three, expect to notice it.

How to brew a smoking blend

This is just tisane practice. Nothing about a blend being rollable changes how you put it in water.

  1. One to two teaspoons per cup. Start at one. These blends are leaf rather than dust, so they take up more room than a tea bag's worth and it is easy to over-measure by eye.
  2. Water just off the boil. Take the kettle off, wait half a minute, then pour. Full rolling boil scorches delicate leaf and pushes bitterness forward.
  3. Cover it. The aromatics you are steeping for are volatile and leave with the steam. A saucer over the cup keeps them in. This is the step most people skip and it makes the largest difference.
  4. Five minutes, then taste. Go to ten if it is too thin. Stop early if there is mugwort in it.
  5. Strain properly. Loose leaf that keeps sitting in the cup keeps extracting, and the last mouthful will not taste like the first.

Preparation guidance from general loose-leaf practice rather than from any specific source. Adjust to taste; none of it is a rule.

If you want to roll it instead

  • Coarse grind, not fine. Airflow is the whole game. Fine material packs, restricts the draw and burns hot.
  • Dry, but not bone dry. Damp blend will not stay lit. Over-dry blend turns to dust and burns too fast.
  • Pack loosely. Herbal blends are lighter than tobacco and need more air, so a tight roll that would be right for a cigarette will be wrong here.
  • Expect it to burn faster. Mullein-heavy blends go quickly. That is normal and not a sign of a bad blend.
